# Concept strategy and evaluation

## Hook procedure
Summarize the video's confirmed promise in one sentence; state the audience's unanswered question. Distinguish proof (video/transcript/source) from conjecture. Identify one of: wow, why, experiment, result, contrast, problem-solution, verification, tutorial.

## Candidate separation
- A: strongest real visual event or result.
- B: question/mystery or mechanism composition.
- C: challenge/verification or comparison composition.
Change framing, focal element, and implied question between A/B/C.

## Score rubric (editorial, 100 points)
| Criterion | Maximum | Measurement |
|---|---:|---|
| One-glance subject recognition | 20 | Recognizable at ~160x90 thumbnail |
| Curiosity without deception | 20 | Clear unresolved question, no false promise |
| Mobile readability | 15 | Clear silhouette, contrast, typography |
| Thumbnail/title complementarity | 15 | Different contributions to same viewing promise |
| Truthfulness / factual match | 15 | Verifiable in source video, no unsupported numbers |
| Channel brand fit | 15 | Matches audience and consistent channel styling |

Scoring is preliminary prior to rendering, then re-score with actual pixels. No model can reliably infer CTR from these values. If factual fit is < 12/15 or there are unlicensed elements, block release pending review.

## Hook copy examples (not factual claims)
Science: 「一滴就爆？」, 「怎麼做到？」, 「真的會開？」.
AI Watch: 「AI 自己做？」, 「成功了？」, 「差在哪？」.
Numbers such as 「省下2小時」 require documented measurements.

## Experimental evaluation
Prefer YouTube Studio's supported A/B testing for eligible long-form videos, measure watch-time-based winner. Also inspect CTR by traffic source, impressions, audience retention, and subscriptions; testing results may be inconclusive. Avoid claiming causality from a simple before/after swap.
